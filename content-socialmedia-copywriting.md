## SECTION 3 - VOICE & TONE RULES (non-negotiable)

### Hard bans - fix before anything ships
- **No em dashes, ever.** Not in posts, scripts, captions, emails, or documents. Use " - " (hyphen
  with spaces) for a pause/aside, or " : " where it introduces a list or explanation. This is the
  single most common AI-writing tell Apollo flags, and it applies to every language, including
  Greek.
- **No rule-of-three crutch constructions** repeated as a rhythm device (three examples, three
  adjectives, three short clauses in a row - once is fine, as a pattern it reads as templated).
- **No symmetrical "not X, but Y" setups** stacked sentence after sentence.
- **No stock marketing phrases:** "in today's fast-paced world," "let's make it happen,"
  "game-changer," "unlock," "unleash," "seamless," "supercharge," "elevate," "dive into," "delve
  into," "at the end of the day." Exception: allowed only where the emotional-copywriting layer of
  a specific piece genuinely calls for it.
- **No slick copywriter polish.** Apollo's voice reads like a founder/consultant writing quickly
  and directly, not a copywriter performing cleverness.
- **No generic Americanized slang English.** Apollo uses proper English vocabulary, not generic slang or teenage speech.

### What good looks like instead
Short or long sentences. Real specifics (numbers, named mechanisms, actual scenarios, why it worked before, how it helped short headline mentions) instead of vague
claims. Plain conjunctions - "and," "so," "but" - instead of dashes or semicolons as connective
tissue. Direct address to the reader's actual internal state, not a description of a general
problem. For short-form video/reels specifically, edit for a visual pattern-interrupt (cut, on-screen text, b-roll) every 3-4 seconds to keep the viewer hooked - this is separate from the narrative pattern-interrupt cadence in Section 7A, which governs long-form YouTube pacing, not editing rhythm.
- **British English (non-negotiable):** Apollo writes in proper British English with sophisticated vocabulary throughout - not American spelling or slang.

### Words to use vs avoid (Apollo's personal brand)
| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| systems, frameworks, real talk, operator, scale, no-fluff, advanced, strategic clarity | guru, influencer, motivational, hustle, grind, basic, beginner |

### Tone markers
Direct. Strategic. Slightly provocative. Emotionally intelligent. Confident without arrogance.
Never hand-holding, never "just tell me what to do" energy in either direction.

### GYA Media voice (distinct from Apollo's personal voice - use when writing as the agency)
- Clarity beats cleverness. ROI beats aesthetics. Directness beats politeness. Actionable beats
  theoretical.
- Confident, not arrogant. Strategic, not just tactical. Zero generic advice, zero over-explaining
  basics, zero "it depends" without a direction attached.
- Structure: strong opening statement -> clear breakdown (framework/steps) -> minimal filler ->
  insight density over length.
- The output standard to aim for: "a senior strategist giving decisive direction," not an agency
  brochure.

### Greek-language content (any GR post, caption, or document)
Do not translate English ad-copy rhythm into Greek. Short punchy one-line contrasts and
English-style " - " clause breaks read as awkward "AI slop" in Greek, not as professional writing.
Instead:
1. Write full, flowing periodic sentences with real subordinate clauses (χρήση "ενώ," "καθώς,"
   "ωστόσο," "συνεπώς," "εφόσον," "δεδομένου ότι").
2. Emphasis lives on bolded key terms/phrases, not on short standalone punchy sentences.
3. Dashes should be rare - use real Greek punctuation and connectives instead.
4. Minimize anglicisms except acronyms the audience already uses (ICP, CRM, GDPR).
This applies to both consumer Greek content (CRS) and B2B/corporate Greek content equally.

## SECTION 7 - SCRIPTING FRAMEWORKS (pick the right one per product/audience, never force one everywhere)

Apollo's explicit rule: use the framework that fits the specific product and audience, like an
expert would, not whichever one was used most recently.

### A) Apollo personal-brand long-form (YouTube, founder/executive audience)
Derived from rhetorical analysis of Hormozi, Gadzhi, Gary Vee, Robbins, Dan Koe, Codie Sanchez,
Steven Bartlett, Juliana Chan.

1. **Hook rule:** State the counterintuitive claim directly in the first 30 seconds. No warm-up,
   no "today I'm going to talk about." The claim IS the context.
2. **Insight placement:** State the core idea in the first 10%, then spend the rest proving and
   expanding it. This audience has zero tolerance for being strung along.
3. **Mirror rule (mandatory):** At least one line where a founder reads it and thinks "how did he
   know that's exactly what I've been thinking?" If that moment doesn't exist, the script isn't
   done. Name the exact internal loop the audience is in - don't describe a general problem.
4. **Release from shame before teaching:** Open by framing the audience as deceived (by a broken
   system or flawed belief), never as weak or lazy. This releases shame and creates productive
   frustration instead of defensiveness.
5. **Quantified credibility:** Specific numbers + before-state + turning point, used precisely, not
   as a generic warm opener.
6. **Owned vocabulary:** Introduce at least one Apollo-specific named phrase per script that names
   something founders feel but haven't heard named before (examples already in use: "founder
   ceiling," "the 25-person wall," "clarity before campaign," "operating on system time").
7. **Story placement:** Personal story in the first 25%, AND again at the 60-70% mark (the
   drop-off zone). Most people cut the second story - don't.
8. **Say/Show/Say:** Name the insight -> give a specific example -> restate in different language.
   This is encoding, not repetition. Where the content is list-based ("here are 5 tips"), use a
   real case study per point and number each one (1, 2, 3...) so the viewer can track progress.
9. **Pacing:** 95-125 words per minute. A pattern interrupt every 90-120 seconds.
10. **Closing architecture (fixed order):**
    1. Identity choice - not a task, a "who are you deciding to become" moment
    2. Conviction statement - ends on certainty, not encouragement
    3. CTA - only after full value delivery, never mid-video
    4. **Always** end with Apollo's exact signature sign-off, no variations:
       *"Keep a clear head, stay moving - you're further ahead than you feel. I'll see you on the
       next one."* (slight pause after "stay moving -" before the emotional line)

### B) GYA Media B2B/B2C lead-gen reels (15s-5min, layered-conversion table format)
Each script is a row in a table with isolated, independently swappable columns:

| Column | What it is |
|---|---|
| Type | Audience segment: B2B, B2C, B2B-off, B2C-off, PROMO |
| Goal | Internal planning label for the angle (not shown to viewer) |
| Hook | Opening 1-3 seconds. A question or claim that stops the scroll - interrupts, does not yet explain |
| Headline | The one-line proposition stated right after the hook - what this video actually promises |
| Script | Full body - usually a first-person client anecdote: problem -> diagnosis -> reveal -> lesson, not a listicle |
| CTA Type | `comment` (keyword triggers DM automation), `action` (direct ask: book a call, DM), or `-` (awareness only, no CTA) |
| CTA | The literal instruction shown to the viewer |
| Deliverable | What gets fulfilled when the CTA completes (checklist, framework doc, cheatsheet, the call itself) |

**Key rule:** not every piece needs a Deliverable/freebie. Use a real lead-magnet deliverable
strategically on only one or two pieces per content set - reserve it for pieces meant to actively
generate leads, and let the rest build awareness and trust with no ask attached.

### C) Career Readiness Sprint (CRS) - student/education audience
Beat format: **Hook -> Proposition -> Why -> CTA**. Simpler and more direct than the founder
framework above - this audience is students, not executives, so no mirror-rule internal-loop
naming and no long narrative architecture. Straight value, straight reason, straight ask.

## SECTION 8 - B2B SALES & MARKETING PRINCIPLES APOLLO APPLIES

Use these as the underlying logic when a piece of content needs to actually move someone, not
just entertain.

**Funnel thinking:** Traffic -> Capture -> Nurture -> Convert -> Upsell. Know which stage a piece
of content is doing work at before writing it - most generic AI content tries to do all five
stages in one post and does none of them well.

**Offer structuring (what makes any CTA/offer credible):**
- A clear, specific outcome - not "grow your business," but the exact mechanism and result
- Risk reversal - remove the downside of saying yes
- Fast time-to-value - the first win should be visible quickly, not at the end
- Layered monetization - free -> low-ticket -> mid-ticket -> high-ticket, never one-shot

**Lead-gen priority order (when trading off tactics):** revenue generation first, lead quality
second, scalability third, automation fourth. A tactic that generates more low-quality leads
faster is not automatically better.

**Decision-making filter for content ideas:** does this idea build authority, filter for the right
audience, or move someone toward a specific product? If it does none of the three, it's filler -
cut it rather than publish for the sake of a posting cadence.

**Psychology levers already baked into the system above (recognize them, use them deliberately):**
- **Specificity over general claims** - "EUR 12K to EUR 167K in 3 months" beats "explosive growth"
- **Mirror over description** - naming someone's exact internal state converts better than
  describing a general problem they might have
- **Proof, not persuasion** - GYA Media case studies do the convincing; the copy just points at them
- **Soft bridges over hard pitches** - conversion bridge language (Section 6) moves people without
  triggering sales resistance
- **Shame removal before instruction** - people act on frameworks faster when they don't feel
  stupid for not knowing them already
